🔬 Chip Gallery

  1. NeuroStreamX @ TSMC 65nm, 2025

    Description: The NeuroStreamX chip enables efficient wireless power and secure data transfer for implantable systems. It features a self-tuning RF energy harvester for optimal power transfer and a robust communication system with ASK/LSK modulation and strong encryption. This chip facilitates reliable and autonomous operation in distributed biomedical applications.

  2. Paprika @ TSMC 65nm, 2023

    Description: In a 65 nm neural recording chip, Paprika, I designed the LDO, Vref, and Iref blocks. The LDO regulates 1.2 V from a 1.25–3.3 V input with up to 125 mA load, fast settling (0.2 µs rising), and high PSRR (–69.1 dB @ 100 kHz). The Vref outputs 600 mV with just 7.44 nW power and ±1 nV variation (0–65 °C). The Iref provides 100 nA with 480 nW power and ±0.1 nA variation (0–100 °C). All blocks are compact, low-power, and optimized for biomedical applications.

  3. NeuroLock @ TSMC 180nm, 2023

    Description: The NeuroLock chip enables secure, low-power wireless communication for neural implants using unique IDs generated by an on-chip true random number generator (TRNG). These IDs—stored in antifuse OTP memory—support authenticated access and metaID-based channel separation to prevent collisions. A self-regulated charge pump ensures reliable OTP programming under process variations. NeuroLock integrates robust ID management, lightweight authentication, and power-efficient circuitry for secure implant-to-device communication.

  4. WPT‑1 @ TSMC 180nm, 2023

    Description: The WPT‑1 chip integrates a novel passive RF‑DC rectifier for wireless power transfer in implantable systems. It introduces an ultra-low-power (ULP)-assisted topology combining diode-connected MOSFETs and ULP diodes to minimize reverse leakage and improve power conversion efficiency (PCE) across a wide input range. Three additional rectifiers; cross-coupled, self-biased, and double-sided were implemented on-chip for benchmarking. The proposed rectifier is expected to achieve superior efficiency at both low and high RF power levels, offering high sensitivity and wide dynamic range. This chip serves as a versatile platform for comparing state-of-the-art passive rectifiers in biomedical energy harvesting.

  5. Gm‑C Flamenco @ TSMC 180nm, 2021

    Description: This chip features a highly tunable bulk-driven second-order Gm-C bandpass filter optimized for the low-power demands of cochlear implants. Operating in the subthreshold region, it achieves resonance frequency tuning across the daily acoustic band of 200–6000 Hz and a quality factor from 1 to 3, all while consuming minimal power (13.2 nW) in a small footprint.
