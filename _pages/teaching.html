    ELEC70012 – Full Custom IC Design Lab
    Imperial College London

               

                    This graduate-level course introduces students to the full-custom design flow of analog integrated circuits using Cadence tools in GPDK 45 nm CMOS technology. The lab is supported by a team of graduate teaching assistants, with my role involving coordination, technical oversight, the development of core materials and lab content.            

               

    Key learning modules include:

               
                     
    • CMOS inverter and ring oscillator design
      •                
    • Voltage-controlled oscillators (VCOs) with enable functionality and post-layout verification
      •                
    • Differential amplifiers and two-stage op-amps with Miller compensation
      •                
    • Monte Carlo and PVT simulations, layout and LVS/DRC verification
      •

    ELEC70093 – Laboratory in ADIC (Advanced Design in Integrated Circuits)
    Imperial College London

               

                    This postgraduate module builds on the foundational concepts introduced in ELEC70012, focusing on more advanced techniques in both analog and digital IC design. Students work with AMS 0.35 µm technology using Cadence tools to complete projects that integrate design, simulation, layout, and verification. The lab is supported by a team of graduate teaching assistants, with my role involving coordination, technical oversight, and the development of core materials and lab content.            

               

    The analog track covers:

               
                     
    • Operational transconductance amplifiers (OTAs), mismatch and noise analysis
      •                
    • Switched-capacitor circuit design with PSS/PAC simulations
      •                
    • Parasitic-aware layout and post-layout verification
      •            
               

    The digital track includes:

               
                     
    • RTL simulation and logic synthesis
      •                
    • Static timing analysis and place & route
      •                
    • GDSII export and design-for-fabrication practices
      •            
           

  3. ELEC40002 – Analysis and Design of Circuits
    Imperial College London

    This foundational module provides an introduction to the analysis and design of electronic circuits for undergraduates. It covers the behavior of core components (resistors, capacitors, inductors, diodes, transistors) and fundamental analysis techniques in both time and frequency domains, including small-signal analysis. Students learn essential methods such as Kirchhoff's laws, nodal analysis, and theorems (Thévenin/Norton), and apply them to basic circuit blocks, including an introduction to SPICE simulation.

    Key topics covered:

    • Basic circuit elements and Kirchhoff’s laws
    • Nodal analysis, Superposition, Thévenin & Norton theorems
    • AC circuit analysis using phasors, Transfer functions & filters
    • Diodes, BJTs, MOSFETs: large-signal and small-signal analysis
    • SPICE simulation
    • Basic transistor circuits (amplifiers, logic gates)
    • Operational amplifiers and feedback
    • Transient analysis of 1st order RC/RL circuits

    As a graduate teaching assistant for this module, I supported student learning by attending problem-solving classes and delivering laboratory sessions.
