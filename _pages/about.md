# Berkay Özbek

I am a PhD candidate in the Next Generation Neural Interfaces (NGNI) Lab at **Imperial College London**, where I have been developing low‑power analog and mixed‑signal integrated circuits for biomedical implants. My work focuses on architecting secure, ultra‑efficient wireless power and data transfer systems that enable minimally invasive, distributed neural interfaces, propelling innovations in implantable neural interface technologies.

## Research Overview
My research portfolio encompasses four key domains:

- **RF Energy Harvesting & Power Management.** Development of adaptive rectifier architectures with absolute peak detection to optimize RF‑to‑DC conversion efficiency under variable loading and misalignment. Designed comprehensive power management units (PMUs), including low‑dropout regulators (LDOs), bandgap references (BGRs), voltage/reference generators (Vref & Iref), and integrated communication front‑ends (clock/data recovery circuits, ASK/LSK modulators and demodulators, Manchester encoders/decoders).
- **Adaptive Impedance Matching.** Real‑time dynamic tuning of matching networks to maintain optimal power transfer across fluctuating implant orientations and heterogeneous tissue impedances.
- **Secure & Robust Wireless Communication.** Implementation of lightweight authentication protocols, autonomous true random number generators (TRNGs), charge pumps, and antifuse one‑time programmable (OTP) cells for secure identifier assignment and encrypted implant‑to‑host telemetry.
- **Low‑Power Analog Front‑Ends.** Design of ultra‑low‑noise analog front‑end circuits —including low‑noise amplifiers (LNAs), analog filters, and variable‑gain amplifiers (VGAs)—achieving single‑digit microwatt operation, as demonstrated in my MSc research on programmable cochlear interfaces.
